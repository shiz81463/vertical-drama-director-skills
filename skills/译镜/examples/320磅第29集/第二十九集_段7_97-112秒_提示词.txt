【实拍光影规范——高优先级】
PRACTICAL EFFECTS ONLY. Every effect must look physically shot in camera on a film set on 35mm film, never added in post.
Every light lights the faces near it, the light falling off into deep darkness. Nothing glows without lighting the world around it. In the show hall the house lights are down: light comes only from the white wash on the runway, the warm gold wash on the backdrop and, while Wendy speaks, one soft-edged spotlight on her; a light haze shows the rays of light. Every face is lit the way a lead is lit in a high-end drama: a large, soft key from a light that is really in the scene, the shadow side of the face falling away cleanly into shade, a thin edge of light along the hair and shoulders that lifts the person off the background, and a clear catchlight in each eye. The eye always goes to the faces first: they are the most carefully lit thing in the frame, the background falls away behind them into soft focus, and the blacks are deep and clean yet still hold detail.
FACING 1 (toward the arched backdrop): white light from the truss falls on everyone on the runway from above and in front; Wendy stands in a soft-edged spotlight that comes from behind the camera; the backdrop glows warm gold; the audience on both sides stays dark; no lamp is in frame, only soft rays in the haze.
FACING 2 (toward the audience): the light comes from behind the camera, from the stage, and softly lights the faces in the first rows warm, each row darker than the last; a few small lamps high at the far end shine toward the camera, small and never over a face.
FACING 3 (toward the front row): the bright runway edge bounces soft white light up into the front-row faces from below and in front; the stage end is at screen-RIGHT and its warm glow rims the hair on that side; the rows behind sink into darkness.
Air: a light haze hangs in the light over the runway; shoot through the blurred edge of a front-row chair or of the arch along one side of the frame so that everything feels photographed in one real space.
Real lens and real film: highlights bloom and halate with a red-orange halo, slight chromatic fringing, softer focus at the frame edges, heavy motion blur and step-printed smearing on fast action, film grain and faint scratches; the stage-light highlights roll off into a soft, creamy bloom while the shadows sink into a deep, clean teal-blue darkness.
Restraint: a few large, motivated effects rather than many small ones. Every effect has a physical cause and a physical consequence.

EYELINES: Wendy looks out over the audience just past the lens; Ethan looks screen-RIGHT and up at her. In singles each eyeline passes just beside the lens. Every look has a target in the scene.
【戏的逻辑——高优先级】
SCENE LOGIC: The last words land on everyone at once. Ethan, who once judged her by her size, is the proudest person in the room.
【镜头衔接——高优先级】
FOUR SHOTS, hard cuts only at 6, 9 and 12 s. Every cut has a reason (a look, a line, a reaction, a movement); each shot opens exactly where the last one ended, and no line and no action restarts after a cut.
TALL FRAME: the frame is tall and narrow. Wendy stands in it with the arch behind her; seen from behind her, she is a small figure low in the frame with the whole room above. Faces stay in the upper two thirds of the frame; anyone who comes or goes moves toward or away from the lens.
UPRIGHT: the camera is always level and the right way up: floor at the bottom, gravity pulling down, heads above feet.
ONE IMAGE: every shot is one continuous image of one real space through one lens: no split screen, no panels, no picture in picture, no double exposure.
SHOT AND REVERSE SHOT: Wendy from the front with the arch behind her; the room from high behind her; Ethan across the runway with the rows behind him.
CAMERA FEELS: the camera moves only when a feeling changes, eases in as someone gives themselves away, and holds still while a feeling is held in; it never zooms and never cuts away before a reaction lands.
