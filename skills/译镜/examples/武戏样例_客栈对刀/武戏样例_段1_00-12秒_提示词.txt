【实拍特效规范——高优先级】
PRACTICAL EFFECTS ONLY. Every effect must look physically shot in camera on a film set on 35mm film, never added in post.
1. The hall is dim between the lamps. Light comes only from the oil lamps on the tables and the lanterns on the far wall. Every flame and spark lights its surroundings with an uneven flicker: faces, skin, steel, dust and the air itself, the light falling off into deep darkness. Nothing glows without lighting the world around it. Every face is lit the way a lead is lit in a high-end drama: a large, soft key from a light that is really in the scene, the shadow side of the face falling away cleanly into shade, a thin edge of light along the hair and shoulders that lifts the person off the background, and a clear catchlight in each eye. The eye always goes to the faces first: they are the most carefully lit thing in the frame, the background falls away behind them into soft focus, and the blacks are deep and clean yet still hold detail.
FACING 1 (toward the far wall): the oil lamps light every face from screen-LEFT, warm and uneven; the lanterns on the far wall glow small in the depth; no bare flame is close to the lens.
FACING 2 (toward the door and the overturned table): the oil lamps light every face from screen-RIGHT, warm and uneven; the door behind stays dark.
2. Matter in the air: lamp smoke and dust hang in the lamplight the whole time; every fast move throws them up, and every impact throws a burst of dust along with whatever it breaks loose. Dust drifts between the camera and the fighters, some of it right across the lens. Shoot through foreground layers (the edge of a pillar down one side of the frame) so that everything feels photographed in one real space.
3. Air: when a body or a blade moves fast, a visible push of air goes ahead of it, scattering the dust and bending the lamp flames; hair and cloth are pressed flat before the blow lands: the air arrives before the blow.
4. Each kind of contact has its own effect. Sparks come only where metal strikes metal: a short burst of separate sparks of uneven size that fly apart in arcs under gravity, cool from white to orange to dull red and die on the floorboards. A blow to the body knocks a burst of sweat and dust out of the cloth. A blade in wood throws splinters.
5. The saber and sword slashes: plain worn steel. Every swing tears through the lamp smoke along its arc and leaves a broad smear of it filling the whole area the blade sweeps, dense at the leading edge, gone within a third of a second; never a thin line.
6. Real lens and real film: highlights bloom and halate with a red-orange halo, slight chromatic fringing, softer focus at the frame edges, heavy motion blur and step-printed smearing on fast action, film grain and faint scratches; the lamp highlights roll off into a soft, creamy bloom while the shadows sink into a deep, clean teal-blue darkness.
7. Restraint: a few large, motivated effects rather than many small ones. Every effect has a physical cause and a physical consequence.

【打斗——最高优先级】
ENGLISH ONLY: every spoken word is in American English, never Chinese; background people make only wordless sounds (gasps, breath, grunts).
TONE: fierce and fast from the first frame, everything at real speed; hit-stops last only a few frames and snap back to full speed.
FIGHT LOGIC: The bandit's long saber needs two paces; the swordsman's straight sword only works inside one. The swordsman must get inside the saber's reach; only a blow to the ribs counts. Each move is forced by the last: the table blocks his retreat, the overswung saber opens the ribs, the saber stuck in the pillar leaves the bandit unguarded.
Every named character appears exactly once, and no extra looks like any of them.
EVERY EXCHANGE is complete: intent, attack, dodge or block, impact, body reaction, loss of balance, change of position, next move; the momentum of one move starts the next. No pause lasts longer than half a second. Weapon contact lasts 2 to 4 frames, then they part.
REAL HIT REACTIONS, VIOLENT, each heavier than the last: first the final acceleration, robes and hair dragged back, air pressed ahead of the blow; then the contact, 2 to 4 frames of hit-stop, then sweat and dust blasting outward and the camera knocked hard; then the transfer of force: the point of contact moves first, the body folds around it, the head whips after it; the bandit is hurled far, away from whoever hit them, crashing through a table. The face of whoever is hit shows the pain. Nobody recovers instantly.
FACES: Swordsman (剑客): calm and exact; his eyes read the blade before he moves. Bandit (山匪): loud and sure of his reach; it shows in his wide swings. Before every big move a held breath and a set jaw; whoever is hit shows it in three beats: the breath knocked out, the pain on the face, then pushing through or going down; fear keeps the eyes on the threat. Effort and pain make only wordless sounds: grunts and breath.
【镜头衔接——高优先级】
FOUR SHOTS, hard cuts only at 3, 5 and 8 s. Every cut is matched on action: each shot opens by continuing the exact movement the last shot ended on, in the same direction, in the same place and light; no action restarts after a cut. 180-degree rule: the swordsman is always on the LEFT, the bandit on the RIGHT; the bandit attacks toward the lens, the swordsman attacks into the depth of the hall; anyone who is hit flies away from whoever hit them. Never cross the axis. Every shot is a new moment from a new angle; nothing is shown twice.
TALL FRAME: the frame is tall and narrow. The two fighters are not spread across its width: when both are in frame, both stand on the same floor at their real heights, the nearer one larger, or one is above the other. Faces and every point of contact stay in the upper two thirds of the frame. Movement that must be read, a rush, a throw, a fall, runs along the length of the frame, toward or away from the lens or down its height, rather than across its narrow width.
UPRIGHT: the camera is always level and the right way up: the floor at the bottom of the frame, gravity pulling down, heads above feet, even while the camera is knocked and tumbles.
ONE IMAGE: every shot is one continuous image of one real space through one lens: no split screen, no panels, no picture in picture, no double exposure.
VISUAL TENSION: the camera never holds still for more than half a second and never lingers on a face: it moves with the action, rushes in physically, never zooming, whips to each new threat a beat late, is knocked by every blow and tumbles with whoever is thrown.
SPEED IS RELATIVE: when the camera follows a fighter, it keeps pace with them, so they stay sharp while the world tears past: whatever is within a few meters, pillars, lamp flames and flying splinters, flashes past in two or three frames as streaks; the far wall and its lanterns smears along the line of movement; the far background barely moves. The camera only just keeps up: on every turn it lags, overshoots and catches up. Speed leaves traces: the wake of each rush tears the lamp smoke into curls.
